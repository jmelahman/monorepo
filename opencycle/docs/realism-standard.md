# The neon street: what counts as real

The direction is an underworld, not a lively market: rundown brutalist concrete under a
deck, closer to Altered Carbon, Star Wars' lower levels and Nivalis Nights than to Mong Kok.
Mostly shut, mostly dark, with a few signs that each count. Where the sections below talk
about full shops and overlapping signs, this wins.

The goal is a street that **feels** real, not one that could pass for footage. Lighting
doesn't have to be accurate, but it has to be high fidelity: rich, deliberate and full of
detail. An interesting building beats a plausible one. This replaces an earlier standard
that scored frames against photos of Mong Kok, which pushed the street towards being bright
and dull.

The photos are still useful for how a street is put together, not for how it should look:

- [Shantung Street neon signs](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mong_Kok_Neon_Signs_Night_(48127904386).jpg).
- [Mong Kok nightlife 2](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mong_Kok_nightlife_2.jpg).

## What to keep from the photos

- **Signs face the viewer.** They hang out over the road on steel frames and overlap.
- **Sign text is real**: big readable characters over small print and phone numbers.
- **Shops are full**, with goods spilling out onto the pavement.
- **Everything is attached to something**: poles, frames, brackets, railings, conduit.
- **The street is messy**: crates, road signs, cracked patches, painted arrows.

## What to throw out

- Accurate exposure. Blacks stay black, and a sign may light a wall no real sign would.
- A merely damp road. A mirror of colour is worth having, whatever the weather was.
- Ordinary buildings, where something stranger would make the view worth riding through.

## Reading a frame

There is no score, because a total can pass a frame nobody would want to look at. Ask:

1. **Would you stop to look?** There should be one thing in the frame worth pointing at.
2. **Is there depth?** Near, middle and far should separate, and the street should keep going.
3. **Does it look up?** The city overhead is half the view, and it should be more than a wall.
4. **Is there contrast?** Real blacks, with air that adds depth and never washes out the frame.
5. **Do colours fight?** Cool against hot. A frame that's all one pink has failed.
6. **Is anything alive?** Rain, steam, moving screens, something passing overhead.
7. **Does it hold up close?** Nothing near the camera should be a flat box or a blurry texture.

## Checking motion

Stills lie. A capture is also checked for shimmer on signs and window grids, popping as
things appear, flicker in reflections, and anything repeating in time.

## The real test

Someone who hasn't seen the project watches a minute of the ride. Do they want to keep
riding? Everything above only predicts this result.
